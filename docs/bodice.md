# Bodice Drafting Guide

This is how Pattern Studio drafts a basic front bodice. The example sizes come from the **Zanira Clothing** size chart.

All measurements are in **inches**.

> ⚠️ Values marked *(est.)* are starting estimates graded from the Small example. Check them against a real fitting before using them for production.

---

## 1. Why we divide by 2 and by 4

A top is the same on the left and the right. You don't draw the whole body. You fold the fabric in half, draw **one half**, and cut through both layers together. When you open the fabric, you have the full piece.

**Bust ÷ 4**
- The bust tape goes all the way around the body, front and back.
- Half of that is the front and half is the back, so **÷ 2**.
- The front piece is cut on the fold, so you only draw half of the front, which is **÷ 2 again**.
- ÷ 2 and then ÷ 2 again is the same as **÷ 4**. You draw one quarter of the bust, and the fold and the back piece give you the rest.

**Shoulder ÷ 2**
- Shoulder width is measured from one shoulder tip to the other, straight across the back.
- On the fold, you only draw from the centre (the fold line) to **one** shoulder tip.
- Centre to one tip is half the width, so **÷ 2**.

**Neck width**
- Neck width is also measured from the fold. A front neck width of 3½" gives a finished opening of **7"** across.

**Waist ÷ 4**
- Same reason as the bust: the waist goes all the way around, and you only draw one quarter of it.

> Think of it like a butterfly: you draw one wing, and the fold makes the other one.

---

## 2. Ease

Ease is the extra room added so the wearer can breathe, sit and move. A bodice drafted at the exact body measurement would be skin-tight.

- Bust: add about **½" per quarter** (2" in total around the body).

---

## 3. Measurements and how to get them

### Bodice length
- Measure from the shoulder, at the side of the neck, down to where you want the top to end.
- **Example:** 18" body length + **1" hem** = **19" cutting length**.

### Armhole depth (three ways)

The armhole depth is how far down the armpit sits from the shoulder line. There are three ways to work it out, and each one checks the others. They should give answers within about ½" of each other.

| # | Method | What you need | Formula |
|---|--------|---------------|---------|
| 1 | Arm method | Arm circumference at the armpit joint | arm ÷ 2 + 1 |
| 2 | Bust method | Bust | bust ÷ 4 − 1½ |
| 3 | Direct method | Lower neck to lower armpit | measurement − 1 |

- For a **standard size**, use method 2, because the size chart only gives the bust.
- For a **client**, use method 3 (or 1) if you have the extra measurement, and use method 2 as a check.

### Shoulder to bust point
- Measure from the shoulder (at the side of the neck) down to the fullest point of the bust (the nipple).
- This tells you how far down to place the bust point and the dart point.

### Bust point to bust point
- Measure straight across, from one bust point to the other.
- **Divide by 2** because of the fold. The result is how far the bust point sits from centre front.

### Neckline: scoop

| | Width (from fold) | Depth |
|-|-------------------|-------|
| Front | 3½" | 7" |
| Back | 3½" | 1½" |

> The front and back use the **same width** (3½"), so the front and back shoulder seams are the same length and line up when sewn. Only the depth changes: the front scoops lower than the back.

### Armhole curve
The armhole runs from the shoulder tip down to the underarm. A straight line between those two points would not fit around the arm, so the armhole **curves inward** from that line. The value below is how far the curve goes in at its deepest point.

| Size | Curve in |
|------|----------|
| S | 1" |
| M | ¾" |
| L | ½" |
| XL | ¼" |

> The curve gets **¼" smaller with each size**.

## 4. Zanira size chart (source)

| Size | UK | Bust | Waist | Hips |
|------|----|------|-------|------|
| S | 6–8 | 34–36 | 27–28 | 38–39 |
| M | 8–10 | 37–38 | 28–30 | 40–41 |
| L | 12–14 | 39–40 | 32–34 | 43–44 |
| XL | 14–16 | 42–43 | 35–36 | 45–46 |

**Draft at the middle of each range.** For example, Small has a bust of 34–36, so draft at 35.

## 5. Bodice values per size

Front bodice values. Every width is a **quarter** (bust and waist) or a **half** (shoulder and bust points) because the piece is cut on the fold.

| Size | Draft bust | Bust ÷ 4 | + ease (½") | Armhole depth (bust ÷ 4 − 1½) | Draft waist | Waist ÷ 4 |
|------|-----------|----------|-------------|-------------------------------|-------------|-----------|
| S | 35 | 8¾ | **9¼** | 7¼ | 27½ | 6⅞ |
| M | 37½ | 9⅜ | **9⅞** | 7⅞ | 29 | 7¼ |
| L | 39½ | 9⅞ | **10⅜** | 8⅜ | 33 | 8¼ |
| XL | 42½ | 10⅝ | **11⅛** | 9⅛ | 35½ | 8⅞ |

| Size | Shoulder to bust point *(est.)* | Bust point to bust point *(est.)* | From centre front (÷ 2) | Shoulder width *(est.)* | Half shoulder (÷ 2) |
|------|------|------|------|------|------|
| S | 10 | 7¼ | 3⅝ | 14½ | 7¼ |
| M | 10½ | 7½ | 3¾ | 15 | 7½ |
| L | 10¾ | 7¾ | 3⅞ | 15½ | 7¾ |
| XL | 11¼ | 8¼ | 4⅛ | 16 | 8 |

The Small values follow the reference (shoulder to bust point 9¾–10¼", bust point to bust point 7–7½"). The other sizes are graded by about ¼–½" per size.

### Notes on the source chart
- **XS is left out for now** and will be added later.
- **XXL is left out for now** because its jumps in bust (+4") and waist (+2") don't follow the other sizes.
- **L hips** skip 42. This may be intentional.
